# What Bin? council pilot

## The resident problem

Collection information is often spread across a postcode router, an address picker, separate disruption pages and a different missed-bin form. Residents make avoidable calls when they cannot quickly answer:

- What goes out tonight?
- Has the date changed?
- What belongs in this bin?
- Where can I take an item that cannot go in a household bin?
- Is a missed collection eligible to report?

## The offer

**Reduce avoidable contact, improve recycling accuracy and make resident reporting easier.**

What Bin Is It Tonight? provides:

- exact-address collection lookup from an approved live source;
- collection-night, collection-morning and service-change notifications;
- locally cautious recycling guidance;
- recycling-centre and drop-off information;
- a guided missed-bin route that never claims submission before the council confirms it;
- optional disruption publishing and council announcements;
- privacy-preserving pilot measures.

The app does not fabricate dates, sell address data or place a paid waste service above a free council or charity option.

## Recommended first pilot

- One council area
- 12 weeks after technical onboarding
- Resident app and installable web app
- Approved collection feed or connector
- Weekly source-health review
- Fortnightly service review
- Agreed baseline and success measures before launch

### Pilot stages

1. **Discovery — 1 to 2 weeks:** confirm data owner, address key, collection source, disruption process, report route, accessibility and information-governance contacts.
2. **Integration — 2 to 6 weeks:** connect the approved feed, validate exact properties, map bin labels and colours, test date changes and failure states.
3. **Controlled release — 2 weeks:** council staff and selected residents test real addresses.
4. **Resident pilot — 12 weeks:** monitor accuracy, lookup completion, notification opt-in, failed lookups and support themes.
5. **Decision:** expand, integrate further or close the pilot with an export of agreed results.

## What the council supplies

- an accountable service owner;
- an information-governance contact;
- the approved collection source and property identifier;
- official bin names, accepted-material guidance and service URLs;
- the missed-bin policy and reporting window;
- disruption and bank-holiday update ownership;
- a controlled group of test addresses that contains no unnecessary personal data.

## What is ready now

- iOS, Android and installable web app;
- UK postcode-to-authority routing for the documented 361-authority snapshot;
- exact-property selection;
- normalized collection, address and services contracts;
- a server-side partner connector registry with credentials kept out of the app;
- local reminders, missed-bin tracking, collection history and source labelling;
- public privacy, terms, data-source and support pages;
- automated web, native-export, gateway and store-readiness checks.

## What remains council-specific

- approval to use the council source;
- feed credentials or connector access;
- council branding and content sign-off;
- baseline contact-volume data if call reduction is to be measured;
- DPA/DPIA and service levels appropriate to the agreed integration.
